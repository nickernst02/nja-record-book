Stories we keep telling

NJA lore

Things that happened in this league that nobody who was there has been allowed to forget.

14-0and fourth place

Jason, 2022

Perfect regular season. Most points in the league. First overall seed. Then he lost the semifinal 96.66-141.38 to Jeremy, lost the third-place game to Ross, and finished the year in fourth. The only unbeaten regular season in twenty-one years and it bought him nothing. He has still never won this league.

The most dominant season ever played

Rough Riders, 2007. Went 11-1-1 and scored 138.4 a game in a league that averaged 98.6. That is 40% above league average, and nobody in the other twenty seasons has cleared 33%. Two of the three biggest single weeks in league history relative to their era belong to this team: 185 in Week 9 and 180 in Week 3. They took the 1-seed, beat Chris in the semifinal, and beat Jeremy 130-116 in the final. Mitch Moore will forever sit in the NJA Hall of Fame.

The original Cinderella

Cyclones, 2006. Finished 8-4, drew the 6-seed, the lowest in the field, and then beat the 3, the 2 and the 4 on the way to the title. Only one other 6-seed has ever won this league. His name was Chris Huston, and we will forever remember it, because it is engraved on the trophy.

Jeremy's first two heartbreaks

Jeremy lost the final in 2006 and again in 2007, back to back, before losing two more in 2014 and 2022. Four finals, no rings. The 2006 run is the cruel one: he knocked out the 11-1 top seed to get there and then scored 64 in the final.

The lost years

This league is older than the record book. Ross started it in 2003 under a different name, and those two seasons are gone. No standings, no scores, no champion. Everything on this page begins in 2005 because that is where the paper trail begins, not where the league does. Yahoo also stopped attaching manager names to teams before 2009, so 2005 through 2008 sit outside every career number here. None of it is outside the lore.

Odds and ends

Things the spreadsheet noticed

  • Playoff seeding barely matters. Six titles from the 1-seed, six from the 2, and five from the 5. The 3-seed has never won this league once in 21 tries.
  • Jordan is Mr. December. A .473 career regular-season manager with an 11-3 playoff record and four rings. Nobody else is close to that gap.
  • Jeremy has the best résumé with nothing to show for it. Eleven playoff berths, four finals in 2006, 2007, 2014 and 2022, three third-place finishes, zero titles, and a 7-16 playoff record since 2009.
  • Bo's one ring is getting much heavier around his neck. A .438 career mark, the worst average finish of any 17-year manager (9.4), and a 2011 championship that has to carry all of it.
  • The long walks. Streaks run across seasons, and two are ugly. Nick lost 13 straight regular-season games from Week 13 of 2021 to Week 11 of 2022. Trevor lost his entire 0-13 season in 2010, sat out 2011, then lost the first two of 2012, which is 15 consecutive games he actually showed up for.
  • Nobody is safe. Jack has two titles and three last-place finishes. Jason has four last-place finishes and a perfect season.
  • The unluckiest year on record belongs to Nick in 2020. He scored the second-most points in the league and went 5-8, missing a six-team playoff entirely. Bo did the same thing in 2013, second in scoring at 6-7. Nobody has ever finished first in points and missed.
  • Ties still happen. Thirteen games in 21 years have ended level, including a 118.90-118.90 between Sean and Matt in 2016.
  • Chris is the league's constant. Thirteen playoff berths in 17 years, more wins than anyone, four rings across 2005, 2008, 2017 and 2021, and four runner-up finishes, also more than anyone.
